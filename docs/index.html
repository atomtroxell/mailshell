Your email, in a window of its own.

MailShell gives Gmail and Exchange / Microsoft 365 a proper home outside your browser, on Windows, Mac and Linux. Same account, same mail, nothing to set up or import. Just a cleaner layout, a window you can't lose among your tabs and email links that finally open somewhere useful.

Why not just keep a tab open?

Gmail has never had a desktop app, and a lot of Microsoft 365 work and school plans only include mail on the web. So your inbox ends up as one tab among twenty, easy to lose and easy to close by accident.

Full mail programs fix that, but they bring their own setup, their own sync and a pile of features you didn't ask for. MailShell keeps it simple. It opens your real web mail in its own window and adds the desktop parts a tab can't do.

What you get

A tidier inbox

Gmail gets a shorter header, two-line message rows, labels as small color dots and no Upgrade, Support or Studio buttons. You can switch that off in Settings if you prefer the original. In Exchange, the Groups folder and Microsoft's promotions are hidden and the ribbon is a little smaller.

Gmail only

Every attachment in one place

Open a conversation and every file in it shows up in a bar above the messages. No more scrolling a long thread hunting for one PDF. PDFs and images open in your browser, and everything else saves straight to your Downloads folder.

Email links open in MailShell

Make MailShell your default email app, and clicking an email address on a website opens a new message in its own window, already addressed. Hit Send and the window closes itself.

Nothing to set up

Pick Gmail or Exchange the first time it opens and sign in the way you would in a browser, including through your company's sign-in page. Your labels, folders, filters and settings are already there. There's nothing to import or sync.

Gmail only

Light or dark, your choice

Pick light, dark or your system setting, and Gmail switches to match. Message bodies stay light, so emails still look the way they were sent. Exchange follows your system setting on its own.

Behaves like a real app

It gets its own icon on the taskbar or Dock, with an unread count. Close the window and it keeps running in the system tray (the menu bar on a Mac), so new mail still comes in.

Your mail stays yours

MailShell has no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no servers of its own.

Stays on your computerYour sign-in and settings are saved in MailShell's app data folder on your own machine. There's no MailShell server for your mail to pass through.
No trackingIt doesn't phone home. It only talks to Google or Microsoft (and your company's sign-in page, if you have one), the same way your browser does.

Questions

Is MailShell really free?

Yes. It's free and open source under the MIT License, with no ads, no paid tier and no trial. If you'd like to support it, you can buy me a coffee, but it's entirely optional.

Which accounts work?

Gmail, including Google Workspace accounts, and Exchange work or school accounts on Microsoft 365. Personal Outlook.com and Hotmail accounts aren't supported yet.

Can I use more than one account?

One at a time for now. You can switch between Gmail and Exchange in Settings under Mail service, and MailShell restarts with the new one. Multiple accounts in tabs is next on the list.

What's next

Multiple accounts come first. The rest is planned, in no set order.
