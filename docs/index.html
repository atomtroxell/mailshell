Gmail and Exchange, in a desktop app of their own.

MailShell puts Gmail or Exchange / Microsoft 365 in its own window on Windows, Mac and Linux, with unread counts on your taskbar, notifications from real senders, and a calmer layout. No browser tab to lose.

Everything you need from your mail, nothing you don't

MailShell handles the parts of email you use all day, then gets out of the way.

Unread count on your taskbar

See how many messages are waiting without switching windows. The count shows on the MailShell icon in the Windows taskbar, the Mac Dock, and Linux launchers that support badges.

Notifications that tell you who wrote

When mail arrives while you're in another app, a notification shows the sender and subject. Click it to jump straight to MailShell. Turn these off any time in your system's notification settings.

Gmail only

Every attachment in one place

MailShell lists every file in a conversation in a bar above the messages, so you never scroll through a long thread to find one. PDFs and images open in your browser; other files save to your Downloads folder.

Keeps running in the background

Close the window and MailShell stays in the system tray (the menu bar on Mac), so you still get new mail. Choose Quit MailShell from the tray menu to close it fully.

Light, dark or match your system

Pick a theme in Settings, and your mail follows it too, even if your computer is set differently. Your inbox matches the theme when Gmail UI presets is on, which is the default.

A cleaner, tighter inbox

In Gmail: a slimmer header and sidebar, two-line message rows, and no Upgrade, Support or Studio buttons. In Exchange: no Groups folder, no Microsoft promotions, and a smaller ribbon. Prefer the original look? Turn off Gmail UI presets in Settings.

Private by design

MailShell is a window onto your mail and nothing more. It has no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no servers of its own.

Stays on your computerYour sign-in and settings are stored in MailShell's app data folder on your own machine. There is no MailShell server for your mail to pass through.
No trackingMailShell never contacts its author. It talks only to Google or Microsoft, exactly as your browser would.

Works on Windows, Mac and Linux

MailShell is an early beta, so expect a few rough edges. Windows has had the most testing; the Mac and Linux builds are newer.

Windows 10 and 11Standard installer. The most tested build.
MacBuilt for Intel and runs on Apple Silicon through Rosetta 2. The build isn't signed yet, so macOS blocks it the first time: open it once, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
LinuxAppImage for x86-64. Mark it executable and run it. Behavior can vary between distributions and desktops.

Found a bug? Check open issues or report a new one. Reports from Mac and Linux users are especially helpful right now.

Questions

Is MailShell really free?

Yes. It's free and open source under the MIT License, with no ads, no paid tier and no trial. If you'd like to support it, you can buy me a coffee, but it's entirely optional.

Does MailShell read or store my email?

No. MailShell shows your mail service's own web page in its own window. Your messages stay with Google or Microsoft, and MailShell sends nothing to its author.

Which accounts work?

Two kinds: Gmail accounts, and Exchange work or school accounts (Microsoft 365). Personal Microsoft accounts (Outlook.com, Hotmail) aren't supported yet, but they're on the roadmap, along with Infomaniak and other mail services. Support for more than one account at a time is coming too.

How is this different from a browser tab?

MailShell gets its own taskbar or Dock icon with an unread count, sends sender notifications, keeps running in the tray when you close it, and tidies up the interface. You can't lose it among 40 tabs.

Why does my Mac say it can't open MailShell?

The Mac build isn't signed with an Apple certificate yet. Open it once, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

How do I switch between Gmail and Exchange?

Open Settings, choose Mail service, pick the other one, and restart MailShell.
